Vocabulary blog · WordPress

Lexivanta: a vocabulary blog that is easy to browse

Client
Lexivanta
My role
UI design and WordPress build
Stack
WordPress, Blocksy, Elementor, Figma

The challenge

Lexivanta needed a blog that makes English vocabulary feel organised rather than overwhelming, with a brand that looks trustworthy and reads well on any screen.

The approach

I started in Figma with five core pages: Home, Categories, Single Post, About Us and Contact. The palette came straight from the logo: navy, gold and an off-white paper tone. For type, I paired a high-contrast serif for headings, close in spirit to the wordmark, with Inter for body text.

The solution

The site is built on WordPress with the Blocksy theme. Vocabulary is grouped into seven categories, from linking words and academic words to emotions and everyday work language, so readers can jump straight to what they need. The theme was later converted into Elementor templates for the header, footer and every page, keeping the existing styles and fixing issues along the way.

UI/UX redesign · Shopify

Young Connection: a storefront redesign that keeps what works

Client
Young Connection (Canadian premium menswear)
My role
UI/UX design and front-end build
Stack
Shopify, Liquid, HTML5, CSS3, Swiper

The challenge

The store needed a fresh UI and a clearer shopping path, but the brand's video hero was central to its identity and had to stay exactly as it was.

The approach

I designed a full HTML and CSS mockup first, so the direction could be reviewed before anything changed on the live store. Once approved, the design is converted into the Shopify theme in Liquid.

The solution

The video hero is unchanged. On the landing page, colour and size swatches and the size selector were removed, because the original store doesn't use them and they added clutter. A Swiper slider now shows trust and testimonial content, and the product card has a working multi-image gallery.

Marketing website · Consultancy

Syntrix Consultancy: clear services for business buyers

Client
Syntrix Consultancy Services (my own brand)
My role
Design and development
Stack
HTML5, CSS3, Bootstrap 5.3, AOS, Swiper

The challenge

Businesses looking for web, WordPress or AI-driven software need to understand what a consultancy can do for them in a few seconds. The site had to explain both services and finished products without becoming a catalogue.

The approach

I built the site in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript on Bootstrap 5.3, with a dark teal colour system and a type pairing of Space Grotesk and Inter. AOS adds gentle reveals, and Swiper handles sliders.

The solution

Separate service pages cover web development, WordPress, and AI and no-code software development. Product pages present OrderFlow, a point-of-sale and kitchen display system, and EduCore, a learning management system for schools.

Content management system · Node.js

NOVA CMS: managing a large media catalogue

Project
NOVA CMS (under active development)
My role
Backend and admin features
Stack
Node.js, Express, Supabase, Cloudinary, Sharp

The challenge

A catalogue of TV shows, movies, cast and galleries needs careful data handling, strong SEO controls and images that load quickly on every device.

The approach

A Node.js and Express backend serves the admin tools, with Supabase as the database. Images go through a Sharp pipeline and are uploaded to Cloudinary, which produces WebP files at several breakpoints for responsive srcset delivery.

The solution

TV management covers seasons and episodes, with cast linked through Supabase joins. Movie management is in place, and a dedicated SEO page is wired to REST endpoints that support soft delete, restore and permanent delete. A Supabase row limit that was cutting off the actors table was traced to the PostgREST Max Rows setting and fixed.

Health platform · Full stack

E-Vaccination Management System: one record for every child

Project
E-Vaccination Management System
My role
Full-stack development
Stack
Node.js, Supabase, HTML5, Chart.js, Leaflet, Grid.js, Choices.js

The challenge

Parents, hospitals and administrators each need a different view of the same immunisation records. The platform had to keep those views separate and easy to use.

The approach

Three role-based dashboards sit on a shared Node.js backend and Supabase database: Admin, Parent and Hospital. I chose Axios for requests and dropped jQuery to keep the front end lean.

The solution

Chart.js provides reporting charts. Hospitals are placed by clicking a pin on a Leaflet map rather than typing an address. Grid.js handles data tables, and Choices.js provides searchable dropdowns.

Streaming platform · Static site

Cinelux: a premium streaming brand built for search

Project
Cinelux, rebuilt from the earlier FlickRift site
My role
Brand, design system, front end and SEO
Stack
HTML5, CSS3, Bootstrap 5.3, Swiper, AOS, JSON-LD

The challenge

Turn a free streaming site into a premium brand, with a consistent design and pages that search engines can read and rank.

The approach

The site is fully static with no backend. I built a gold-accented design system and reusable page templates for movies and TV shows, so every new title follows the same proven structure.

The solution

Each movie page has a hero, player, synopsis, cast, recommendations and an FAQ. Multi-season TV pages use a season selector. Every page includes structured data (Movie, TV, breadcrumbs and FAQ), Open Graph and Twitter tags, and hreflang tags for 13 languages.
